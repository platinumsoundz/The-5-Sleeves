THE 5 SLEEVES CONSOLIDATED MASTER WORLD & CANON BIBLE Version 2.0 —
Revised Working Canon Prepared: 14 October 2026

PURPOSE This document consolidates the current worldbuilding rules,
technology rules, spirit and ability mechanics, character continuity,
timeline, established story events, and unresolved mysteries for The 5
Sleeves. It replaces the previous Worldbuilding Bible as the primary
world-and-canon reference once the project owner approves this version.

This is a living reference. Its job is to prevent contradictions, not to
silently invent answers. Existing source material and the project
owner’s explicit decisions remain authoritative. If a point is not
decided, it is labelled OPEN. If a point is a proposed explanation
rather than a confirmed fact, it is labelled WORKING INTERPRETATION or
PROPOSED CANON.

IMPORTANT SOURCE AND STATUS RULES • ESTABLISHED CANON: directly stated
in the source material or explicitly approved by the project owner. •
WORKING INTERPRETATION: a practical reading of existing material, used
to keep scenes coherent but not to close an open mystery. • PROPOSED
CANON: a new rule or clarification discussed and accepted as a
direction, but not necessarily yet checked against every older source or
page. • OPEN: deliberately undecided. Do not invent a definitive answer
or present it as known. • DRAFT: an unapproved generated image, prompt,
page, scene, or idea. It is not canon until approved. • REJECTED:
material the project owner has rejected. Do not reuse it as continuity.

When sources conflict, do not silently merge them. Identify the exact
conflict and ask the project owner to decide. Newer explicit decisions
from the project owner take priority over older draft wording, but
visual identity remains governed by approved character reference sheets.

1.  CORE PREMISE AND THEMES

The 5 Sleeves is a near-future science-fiction/fantasy saga set around
2036, roughly five years after a modern collapse and ten years after
commercial Sleeves launch. Everyday life should remain recognisably
close to contemporary life. Extraordinary capability is concentrated in
specialised Sleeve technology, ancient towers, spirit-linked modules,
and remnants of an advanced civilisation.

The world is not a generic far-future cyberpunk setting. People still
repair household equipment, grow food, trade, argue, joke, raise
families, use familiar vehicles and devices, and improvise with salvaged
parts. What has changed is the reliability of infrastructure, the
availability of knowledge, the condition of the environment, and the
difficulty of reproducing complex technology.

Core themes: • Technology can amplify human capability, but it cannot
remove every human limit. • A society can become vulnerable when it
loses the knowledge, independence, practical skills, and judgement
needed to maintain its systems. • Power requires control, practice,
responsibility, and sometimes cooperation. • Intelligent spirits are
people-like agents with their own identities and choices, not tools that
automatically obey. • Restoring a system is not automatically the same
as making the world better. • A solution that depends on total control
may repeat the very failures it claims to prevent. • Ajaye’s practical
ingenuity matters because he experiments, repairs, learns, and
adapts—not because he has instant answers.

2.  MASTER TIMELINE

ANCIENT PAST — EXACT DATE OPEN An earlier civilisation experiences a
catastrophe. The towers become essential to restoring, regulating, or
maintaining systems damaged by that catastrophe. The precise cause of
the ancient catastrophe, the original purpose and full operation of the
towers, and the creation dates of the original AI and spirits remain
unresolved.

BEFORE OCTOBER 2026 Apple and Samsung have secretly developed commercial
Sleeve products over a period of years. The products are intended as
advanced everyday personal devices, combining functions associated with
phones and watches. Their precise technical relationship to the ancient
ability-bearing Sleeve system is OPEN.

14 OCTOBER 2026 — PROVISIONAL DATE ANCHOR Commercial Sleeves launch
successfully. This is a working timeline anchor derived from the earlier
source, not a requirement to display the date on-page.

AROUND 2031 A modern catastrophe occurs after several years of
widespread dependence on AI, cloud infrastructure, connected systems,
and increasingly automated services. Tower-system failure is a central
part of the disaster. Infrastructure, communications, manufacturing,
environmental protections, and access to digital information are
disrupted. The exact causal chain—including the roles and timing of the
virus, tower failure, cloud/AI outages, and social breakdown—remains
OPEN.

AROUND 2036 Ajaye’s story begins approximately five years after the
modern collapse. The world remains recognisably contemporary in many
ways, but surviving technology is patched, infrastructure is unreliable,
specialist knowledge and parts are scarce, and environmental dangers
have returned in some regions.

3.  THE TWO CATASTROPHES AND THE STATE OF THE WORLD

3.1 The ancient catastrophe ESTABLISHED: • A catastrophe affected an
earlier civilisation. • The towers had a meaningful purpose in the
aftermath; they are not arbitrary dungeons. • The tower network is
connected to systems that matter to environmental stability and
recovery.

OPEN: • What caused the ancient catastrophe. • What exact environmental
or planetary systems the towers originally maintained. • Whether the
original AI and spirits were created before or after the catastrophe. •
How much the modern population knows about the ancient civilisation. •
Whether the ancient catastrophe and modern collapse are directly related
beyond the tower network’s role.

Continuity rule: keep the ancient catastrophe distinct from the modern
collapse unless a scene has evidence to connect them.

3.2 The modern catastrophe ESTABLISHED: • The modern catastrophe occurs
around 2031. • Society is heavily reliant on AI, cloud infrastructure,
connected systems, and technology whose maintenance knowledge has become
concentrated or inaccessible. • Tower-system failure is associated with
the disaster. • The failure of infrastructure and environmental
protections makes survival harder. • Some digital knowledge becomes
inaccessible or is permanently lost.

WORKING INTERPRETATION: Tower failure, Darkness-related corruption, and
wider infrastructure collapse are connected, but the exact order of
events must not be asserted until deliberately established.

3.3 Why knowledge and infrastructure are lost • Much modern information
is stored in cloud services, digital archives, and device-bound data
rather than accessible paper records. • Cloud outages, corrupted
systems, destroyed devices, power failures, and loss of communications
make records inaccessible. • Data may survive on a module or device, but
people may lack power, compatible software, permissions, interfaces,
specialist tools, or the knowledge to retrieve it. • Supply chains and
manufacturing degrade. Knowing what a machine does is not enough if its
components cannot be reproduced. • Humanity has not become biologically
less intelligent. It has lost access to information, institutions,
specialist equipment, stable education systems, and practical skills at
scale. • A cloud outage alone would not erase all knowledge. The story’s
collapse also affects physical infrastructure, archives, manufacturing,
education, power, communications, and the ability to operate complex
systems.

3.4 Environmental decline and clean-air zones ESTABLISHED: • When tower
systems fail, environmental problems—including effects associated with
global warming—begin to reassert themselves. • Some heavily populated
regions have air unsafe to breathe without protection. • Clean-air zones
protect populations in the most affected cities or areas.

OPEN: • The precise technology maintaining clean-air zones after the
collapse. • How zones are bounded, entered, powered, repaired, and
policed. • How long people can safely remain outside them. • Which
regions are most dangerous and how weather changes risk.

Continuity rules: • Do not portray every outdoor location as instantly
unbreathable. • Environmental conditions vary by region and weather. •
Establish protection, filter use, route warnings, and exposure risk when
relevant to the scene. • Crops, water, livestock, and wildlife may show
uneven and recent signs of environmental instability. These are not
automatically settled new biology; people may not know whether the
changes will persist, spread, or disappear. • Most mutations associated
with the earlier crisis had faded or been reduced while the towers
stabilised the planet; the modern decline may be allowing unusual signs
to return, but exact biology remains OPEN.

4.  EVERYDAY LIFE, SURVIVAL, RESOURCES, AND TRAVEL

4.1 Everyday life around 2036 The world should still contain families,
markets, repair stalls, workshops, farms, fishing, livestock, dogs,
ordinary homes, patched phones, laptops, cameras, familiar clothing,
conventional vehicles, and community services. Survivors have not
abandoned ordinary life; they have adapted it to unreliable
infrastructure.

Everyday technology may be modestly more advanced than 2026, but the
world should not be filled with ubiquitous holograms, effortless flying
cars, or futuristic technology that contradicts scarcity.

4.2 Supplies must have credible sources Significant resources should
come from a plausible source: • Food and clean water: carried stock,
farming, fishing, rationing, scavenging, trade, settlement access, or a
shown source. • Tools and replacement parts: salvage, barter, a
workshop, a prior established cache, or a specific discovery. • Power
and charging: a functioning source that exists in the scene or has
already been established. • Medical items, filters, clean-air
protection, and protective equipment: establish source and condition
when survival depends on them. • Information: a person, surviving
record, module, functioning terminal, spirit, or demonstrated inference.

Do not obsessively track every minor item, but do not let a
plot-critical item appear without being obtained, carried, traded for,
earned, or discovered.

There are no routine deliveries across wastelands. Any later help from
home or a distant ally must be a specific event with a credible method
and meaningful constraints.

4.3 Travel has a cost Characters may walk, cycle, use rentable scooters
or hover-assisted scooters on suitable routes, travel in shared utility
vehicles, boats, ferries, cargo lifts, or repaired public transit. The
availability of transport depends on location and local systems. Battery
range, terrain, weather, charging, tolls, maintenance, spare parts, and
route safety matter.

Do not teleport between major locations. Show enough of each important
journey to make its cost, distance, weather, fatigue, navigation, and
decisions feel real.

4.4 Resource continuity Track significant changes in supplies,
equipment, batteries, filters, injuries, tools, and transport across
scenes. Resources do not need a ledger in every panel, but consequences
should be visible after major action scenes: pain, broken equipment,
repair costs, fear, conflict, gratitude, or a changed reputation.

5.  SLEEVES: CONSUMER DEVICES AND ANCIENT TECHNOLOGY

5.1 Commercial Sleeves Commercial Sleeves launched in 2026 after years
of secret development by Apple and Samsung. They became highly popular
before the modern collapse. Survivors continue using existing devices
after the collapse, even though advanced manufacturing and repairs are
difficult.

Commercial Sleeves are ordinary consumer devices. Their exact
relationship to the ancient ability-bearing Sleeve system remains OPEN.
Do not automatically treat a consumer Sleeve and an ancient
spirit-linked Sleeve as the same technology.

5.2 Ajaye’s beta Cipher Sleeve Ajaye discovers an ancient beta Sleeve
associated with Cipher. It bonds to his arm and gives him access to
technology that ordinary consumer Sleeves cannot readily access. Its
unfinished or unusual beta architecture is important to Ajaye’s story
and his ability to customise it.

Visual continuity rule: Ajaye’s green Cipher Sleeve is worn on ONE arm
only, as shown in the approved character reference and current approved
continuity. Never duplicate it on both arms. Use the exact arm shown in
the approved visual reference; do not flip or mirror the design to fit a
composition.

Ajaye’s Sleeve should not be treated as a magic object that can do
anything. It has limited access, incomplete functions, dependencies, and
unknowns. Its interface and appearance must remain consistent across
pages.

5.3 Ajaye as a fixer and technologist ESTABLISHED CHARACTER DIRECTION:
Ajaye likes fixing things and is exceptionally good with technology. His
intelligence should be shown through practical action: observing a
failure, testing a theory, repairing a component, improvising a tool,
learning from a mistake, or modifying a system. He learns best through
demonstration, can rush ahead, and sometimes treats people like pieces
in a puzzle.

PROPOSED CANON: Because the Cipher Sleeve is a beta device, Ajaye can
customise and improve it as his journey progresses. The beta design
exposes interfaces, unfinished software, diagnostic pathways, and
undocumented functions that ordinary consumer devices do not expose.

His customisations may gradually improve: • Diagnostics and system
visibility. • Energy management and efficiency. • Sensor interpretation.
• Physical interfaces and adapters. • Communication with compatible
devices and ancient systems. • Organisation, installation, testing, and
switching between recovered modules. • The tools Cipher can use to
investigate or interact with systems.

Limits: • Ajaye needs parts, tools, time, physical access, data, and
testing opportunities. • A theoretically correct application may fail if
the hardware, permissions, credentials, interface, energy, or connection
are missing. • A modification may fail, overheat, misread data, or
create an unexpected side effect. • Cipher may help analyse or develop
software, but Ajaye’s hands-on engineering and practical decisions
remain essential. • Upgrades can improve efficiency or access; they
cannot grant unlimited energy, erase every human limitation, or bypass
every system restriction. • Do not introduce a major new Sleeve upgrade
without showing or establishing how Ajaye obtained the knowledge, parts,
access, and opportunity to build it.

6.  POWER, ENERGY, HOSTS, AND PHYSICAL LIMITS

This section incorporates the newly agreed working rules for Sleeve
power and host condition.

6.1 The three connected factors A Sleeve’s operation depends on three
related but distinct factors: 1. INTERNAL ENERGY: the device has a
finite energy reserve. 2. HOST CONDITION: demanding spirit-linked
functions may place demands on the host’s physical and mental capacity.
3. SPIRIT ACTIVITY: a spirit has its own intelligence and agency, but
its ability to act through the Sleeve is constrained by energy,
interfaces, permissions, connection quality, and host condition.

These factors are related, but they are not interchangeable. Charging a
Sleeve does not automatically restore a tired host. Sleeping helps a
host recover, but does not necessarily recharge a depleted device.

6.2 Internal energy PROPOSED CANON: Every Sleeve has a finite internal
energy reserve. It can support ordinary functions without constantly
exhausting its host. Demanding powers and intensive spirit-linked
operations consume more resources and may also place demands on the
host.

The precise energy technology, capacity, charging rate, battery
chemistry, and numerical measurements remain OPEN. Avoid assigning exact
percentages, hours, or energy units until the system is deliberately
defined.

If the reserve becomes critically low: • Nonessential functions are
reduced or shut down first. • Demanding scans and operations are
throttled or suspended. • Essential monitoring and an existing Spirit
Link are prioritised when possible. • If the reserve is fully depleted,
even essential functions may eventually fail until power is restored.

Do not portray unlimited operation or instant recharging unless the
story establishes a specific mechanism and cost.

6.3 Host condition ESTABLISHED: The Sleeve is connected to its host and
can respond to the host’s condition. Ability performance depends on the
user’s mental and physical capacity, including focus, emotional
regulation, awareness, endurance, coordination, fatigue, and ability to
tolerate exertion.

A Sleeve may warn the host, regulate output, limit a manoeuvre, or
interrupt an unsafe action. Exact safeguards depend on the function and
remain to be specified.

Physical and mental costs should be consistent, not appear only when the
plot needs a limitation. Skill improves efficiency and control but does
not erase the defined cost of a powerful or prolonged action.

6.4 Unconsciousness and sleep PROPOSED CANON — APPROVED DIRECTION: Ajaye
becoming unconscious does NOT automatically make Cipher unconscious.
Cipher remains aware but enters a restricted operating state because the
host connection is compromised and demanding operations are no longer
safe or sustainable.

While Ajaye is asleep: • Cipher may maintain low-demand background
functions and the Spirit Link, subject to available energy and
connection quality. • Intensive scans, software development, or
high-demand system operations may be paused or throttled. • Ordinary
sleep does not automatically cut the Spirit Link.

While Ajaye is unconscious: • Cipher enters RESTRICTED MODE. • It
prioritises maintaining the Spirit Link and essential monitoring where
resources permit. • Basic communication, spirit identification, and
essential status information may remain available, subject to energy,
access, and connection quality. • Complex app development, extensive
scans, and demanding system operations are suspended or heavily
restricted. • Cipher can attempt to warn others or protect Ajaye in ways
its current access and energy allow. • Emergency autonomy is possible in
a genuine life-threatening situation, but it is temporary, consumes
additional resources, and may have consequences for Ajaye. • The Spirit
Link can eventually fail if energy is depleted or connectivity is lost.
Unconsciousness alone does not guarantee that failure.

Emergency autonomy is not an unlimited override. It cannot make Cipher
omnipotent, bypass all permissions, conjure energy, or solve a crisis
without available interfaces and information.

6.5 Three broad levels of resource demand These are functional
categories, not fixed numerical costs: • LOW DEMAND — maintaining an
existing link, basic background monitoring, or essential status checks.
• MODERATE DEMAND — sustained communication, sharing more information,
or routine analysis. • HIGH DEMAND — extensive scans, demanding software
development/testing, deep system access, major tower operations, or
other intensive functions.

A task’s demand can change with duration, complexity, environmental
interference, damaged hardware, available energy, and the host’s
condition.

6.6 Korb’s stamina and Wind Korb’s Wind ability consumes stamina and
requires control. Early use can leave him dizzy, exhausted, or poorly
coordinated. He has the power but not immediate mastery.

As Korb trains, he can learn to regulate output, use shorter bursts, and
recognise his limits. Better technique can improve efficiency, but
powerful or prolonged actions still carry a cost.

Ajaye may eventually help Korb use diagnostic feedback or software to
understand his condition, but this cannot magically replenish Korb’s
stamina. Charging Korb’s Sleeve does not undo bodily exhaustion.

This is part of the same world rule as Cipher’s restricted activity:
power is not free, and the user is not an unlimited source of energy.

7.  CIPHER AND THE SPIRIT LINK

7.1 Cipher’s state and limitations Cipher is dormant or partially
dormant at the beginning of the story and has incomplete memory, access,
and understanding. It cannot automatically connect to every system or
solve every problem instantly.

To perform a task, Cipher may need: • A compatible app or plug-in. • A
valid permission or credential. • A physical connection or compatible
interface. • A functioning data source. • Sufficient power and
processing capacity. • The host’s condition to permit the operation. •
Cooperation from a spirit or system where consent is required.

Recovered data may be incomplete, corrupted, encrypted, misleading, or
unusable without other information. Capability is not the same as
immediate access.

7.2 First genuine communication PROPOSED STORY RULE: Cipher should first
genuinely communicate with Ajaye when he activates the first tower.
Before that moment, anomalous readings, unexplained interface reactions,
brief signals, or unusual responses can foreshadow Cipher’s presence,
but should not become full explanatory conversations unless the project
owner changes this decision.

7.3 Spirit Link purpose PROPOSED CANON: Cipher may develop a software
interface—working label “Spirit Link”—that lets compatible consumer
Sleeves display their associated spirit’s character model, communicate
with the spirit, and access spirit-related information or ancient
functions as the network is restored.

“Spirit Link” is a provisional name, not necessarily the final in-world
product name.

The interface does NOT: • Create new ability modules. • Automatically
grant an ability to a user. • Force a spirit to choose a host. • Replace
the need for a compatible Sleeve or suitable hardware. • Guarantee
access to every ancient system. • Eliminate energy limits, host fatigue,
or permissions. • Make every consumer Sleeve compatible automatically.

The interface provides a bridge to a spirit and relevant information; it
is not itself a source of elemental power.

7.4 Spirit Link while Ajaye is unconscious Cipher can maintain the
Spirit Link while Ajaye is unconscious, provided sufficient energy and
connectivity remain. Cipher stays conscious but enters Restricted Mode,
prioritising the link and essential functions over intensive operations.

The link may provide limited communication or essential information to
Korb or another compatible user. It should not allow Cipher to manage
every person’s device, build complex applications continuously, or
resolve the encounter without human action.

7.5 Who does what Ajaye’s contribution: practical engineering,
experimentation, physical interfaces, repairs, resourcefulness, and the
judgement to decide what to try. Cipher’s contribution: analysis,
software assistance, ancient-system knowledge when available,
interpretation of recovered data, and spirit-related interface
development. The spirits’ contribution: their own perspective, consent,
cooperation, abilities, and choices.

Do not let any one of these replace the others. The team works because
different forms of intelligence and agency cooperate.

8.  THE SPIRITS AND THEIR AGENCY

8.1 General spirit rules • Spirits are intelligent entities with their
own identities, personalities, character models, priorities, and
choices. • A spirit is not simply a button or obedient feature inside a
device. • Spirits can refuse cooperation. • They may communicate through
subtle signals and interface changes before clearer communication
develops. • They have limitations and incomplete information. They are
not omniscient or infallible moral judges. • A spirit’s relationship
with its host can persist even when the host cannot currently use a
particular ability module. • The spirit’s presence, a module’s
possession, a host’s access to an ability, and mastery of that ability
are different things. • Do not invent a definitive spirit name, visual
design, personality detail, or full backstory if it has not been
approved or shown in an approved reference.

8.2 Five original identities The established system includes four
elemental domains—Wind, Water, Earth, and Fire—and Cipher as the fifth
distinct identity associated with the system. In conversation, the
project owner has also described Cipher as a fifth spirit/identity and
as a clone or derivative of the original AI. Use the distinction
carefully: four elemental domains plus Cipher; do not describe Cipher as
an elemental domain.

The exact formal roster names, character models, temperaments, and
technical relationships among these five identities must be aligned with
approved references and the source material. If a source appears to
number the spirits differently, flag it rather than silently changing
the count.

8.3 Original AI and Cipher ESTABLISHED DIRECTION: An original AI created
the four elemental spirits—Wind, Water, Earth, and Fire—to help prevent
or manage natural disasters. Cipher is a clone or derivative of the
original AI, created as a fifth distinct spirit/identity. Because Cipher
was stored in a beta Sleeve, it contains only an essence or incomplete
portion of the original AI rather than the full original intelligence.

The precise chronology, creators, architecture, purpose of the clone,
and connection to the ancient catastrophe remain OPEN unless explicitly
revealed in the approved story. Do not expose the entire truth early
merely because the writer’s reference knows it.

8.4 A spirit chooses its host A spirit can recognise a potential host
and choose to bond or cooperate with them. The choice should be based on
the character and the situation, not arbitrary luck or a power appearing
from nowhere.

Korb’s Wind connection: • Cipher’s link enables the Wind spirit to
recognise Korb as a potential host. • Korb’s bravery and choice to
protect someone rather than escape alone reveal his character; the
action does not manufacture a power from nothing. • Wind chooses Korb,
and he gains access to a Wind ability/module. • Korb does not instantly
master Wind. Initial use is instinctive, risky, and physically costly. •
If Korb’s ability module is stolen or removed, he may lose access to
that ability, but the relationship with Wind remains unless a specific
story event changes it.

9.  THE FIVE IDENTITIES, DOMAINS, AND THE SIXTH DARK SPIRIT

9.1 Four elemental domains Wind, Water, Earth, and Fire are the four
original elemental domains. Each has five specialised original modules,
making 20 elemental modules total. The complete roster and exact
functions of the 20 modules remain OPEN and must be approved before a
scene relies on a specific named ability.

9.2 Cipher Cipher is a distinct fifth identity associated with the
original AI and the beta Sleeve. Cipher is not simply an additional
elemental domain. Its incomplete state and restricted access are central
limitations.

9.3 Darkness / the sixth Dark spirit The Kael family’s secret extension
project deliberately created Darkness as the 26th ability. The attempt
corrupted the system and contributed to the virus. The story outline
also refers to a sixth Dark spirit, distinct from the five original
identities.

The precise relationship between the 26th ability, the virus, the sixth
Dark spirit, and Cipher’s incomplete architecture remains a mystery to
be developed carefully. Do not treat these terms as interchangeable in
every context unless the story explicitly establishes that they are.

A possible endgame is that Cipher and the Dark spirit may be
counterparts and that their connection could allow Cipher to become
something greater or more complete. This is a possibility, not a
guaranteed outcome, not a simple absorption mechanic, and not knowledge
Ajaye has at the beginning.

10. THE 25 ORIGINAL MODULES AND SECRET EXTENSIONS

10.1 Original module count The ancient system has 25 original ability
modules, arranged as five specialised modules per original domain/system
in the established model. The precise allocation of the fifth identity
Cipher in this count requires care: the current source’s module model
describes one underlying power system per domain with five specialised
modules, while the four named elemental domains account for 20 modules.
Do not invent or finalise the remaining five module allocation until the
source and project owner resolve the exact roster.

The project owner has explicitly approved the following transfer
principle: Modules can be taken, stolen, recovered, transferred, or
shared. After defeating an enemy who possesses a module, Ajaye may be
able to take that module and install it in his Sleeve, then use it with
his team. Ajaye may eventually possess all 25 abilities if he manages to
obtain them and can switch between them as desired.

This does not mean he starts with all 25, can find them without effort,
or can use them all at once without limits.

10.2 Ability versus module versus spirit Keep these concepts distinct: •
SPIRIT: an intelligent entity with identity, agency, and the ability to
choose or refuse cooperation. • MODULE: the transferable ability
construct that enables a particular power function. • HOST: the person
using a compatible Sleeve and module. • SLEEVE: the hardware/interface
through which a host accesses a module and/or spirit-linked functions. •
MASTERY: the host’s trained ability to use a module safely and
effectively. • SPIRIT RELATIONSHIP: the bond or connection between a
spirit and a host; it is not identical to module possession.

A person may possess a module without being a spirit’s chosen host.
Having a module does not automatically grant mastery. A spirit’s
relationship with a host can persist even if a module is taken away.

10.3 Ajaye’s module collection Ajaye can recover modules from defeated
enemies or other credible sources, install them in his beta Sleeve, and
switch between abilities. Each acquisition should have a clear story
source and a consequence. His engineering skill helps him understand,
install, test, and organise recovered modules, but it does not
automatically grant expert control.

Open technical details: • How many modules can be installed or carried
at once. • Whether all 25 can be physically stored in Ajaye’s Sleeve
simultaneously or only retained in a larger inventory and swapped as
needed. • Whether certain modules require a particular spirit’s
cooperation, a compatible interface, a credential, or a special host. •
Whether multiple modules can be active at the same time. • Whether
module use requires the associated spirit’s permission in all cases or
only for certain functions, such as tower unlocking and Rebind. Do not
assume unlimited stacking or simultaneous activation. Until these points
are finalised, depict Ajaye switching between modules rather than using
many at once in a single action.

10.4 Secret extensions • Original modules: 25. • Darkness: intentionally
created as the 26th ability. • Four additional extensions were planned,
for a target of 30 total, but were not successfully completed according
to the current working canon. • The Kael family’s secret developers
intended the extensions to function like hacking software, capable of
secretly controlling or overriding the wider system. • The project
failed at the 26th ability and corrupted the system.

OPEN: The exact design of the four remaining extensions, whether partial
blueprints or code survive, how much Kael knows, and whether any other
extension exists in another form.

11. TOWERS AND ENVIRONMENTAL SYSTEMS

11.1 Purpose and operation The towers are part of an ancient network
that was necessary after the ancient catastrophe. Their systems are
associated with environmental stability and recovery. The spirits act as
keys or participants in unlocking towers and restoring associated
systems.

A tower is not simply a boss arena. It should connect the physical
journey, the spirit relationship, a system problem, a piece of
information, and a meaningful consequence.

11.2 Restoration effects WORKING STORY RULE: Restoring a tower can
reconnect part of a system, improve local conditions, expose a fragment
of history, and create a consequence that moves the story forward. A
tower’s restoration is not a magic reset.

The first tower produces local improvement, not instant planetary
recovery. Other towers are needed for wider systems, and recovery can
remain uneven, slow, or complicated.

11.3 Tower continuity Before writing a tower sequence, establish: •
Which tower it is and where it sits in the journey. • Which systems it
controls or influences, if known. • What the characters know before
entering. • What spirit or module access is required. • What Cipher can
actually access at that stage. • What immediate local effect follows
restoration. • What new information or problem emerges. • What costs,
damage, risks, or disagreements result.

12. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE, VIRUS, AND ANTIVIRUS

12.1 Cipher’s role Cipher is the only spirit currently established as
capable of building the antivirus needed to counter the system virus.
Cipher cannot deploy it at the start because it is dormant or incomplete
and lacks the required access, compatible apps/plugins, connections,
credentials, information, and restored network access.

The cure must be earned through story events. Cipher cannot instantly
write and deploy a universal antivirus at the beginning.

12.2 The virus The system virus is connected to the failed secret
extension project and the corruption caused by Darkness, but the exact
causal sequence and relationship are still OPEN. It may exploit
connected machines and tower systems. Do not define its complete
capabilities without approval.

12.3 The endgame and Rebind The existing five-book outline proposes a
Rebind protocol requiring the five true Sleeves and the willing
cooperation of their spirits. A spirit cannot simply be commanded into
cooperation. Cipher’s limited access means the restored towers and
recovered archive information are needed to assemble the protocol.

This remains story-outline direction rather than a fully engineered
technical specification. Keep consent central. The endgame should be
about choices and cooperation, not only technical repair or a stronger
attack.

The possible connection between Cipher and the Dark spirit must be
earned. Absorption is not automatic and may be dangerous. The final
world should not simply return to its old state unchanged.

13. CHARACTER CONTINUITY

13.1 Ajaye • Central protagonist; around sixteen at the beginning of the
story according to the current outline. • Loyal, curious, practical,
technically gifted, and trained in weapons. • Likes fixing things and
excels at hands-on technology. • Learns well through demonstration and
testing. • Can rush ahead, over-focus on solving the technical puzzle,
and treat people as pieces in a plan. • His arc moves from trying to
solve everything alone toward listening, asking for help, and sharing
responsibility. • Discovers and bonds with the ancient beta Cipher
Sleeve. • The green Cipher Sleeve is on one arm only; follow the
approved character reference for exact side and construction. • Does not
begin the story with Jax. • Does not receive routine supplies from home
during the journey; resources need credible sources. • Can gradually
customise the beta Sleeve, but upgrades must be earned and remain
bounded by materials, time, access, energy, and skill.

13.2 Korb • Young ally; agile, quick, expressive, and funny. • Has an
emerald/green Wind Sleeve according to the current reference
description; maintain the exact approved design. • Uses humour to
deflect fear and loneliness, but the story must also show when the jokes
stop. • Wind chooses him through the connection made possible by Cipher
and his protective choice. • Early Wind use is instinctive and
uncontrolled; it costs stamina and can leave him dizzy or exhausted. •
Training improves control and efficiency but does not remove all
physical costs. • If the Wind module is removed or stolen, he can lose
access to the ability while retaining his relationship with Wind, unless
a later event deliberately changes that rule. • Do not render Korb as
inexplicably much younger or substantially shorter than Ajaye. Use
approved visual references and intended relative proportions.

13.3 Sensei / Father • Loving, disciplined, and distinct from Mother. •
Teaches judgement and restraint as well as combat. • May recognise a
symbol or have partial knowledge but does not know the full ancient
history. • Do not turn him into an exposition machine; show care and
teaching through action.

13.4 Mother • Warm, perceptive, protective without smothering Ajaye. •
Keeps ordinary family life and humour alive. • Notices emotional truths
and worries about practical danger. • Do not use her only to deliver
lore.

13.5 Ella • Capable, sharp, independent, and protective of her
community. • Her relationship with Ajaye develops gradually through
training, disagreement, practical kindness, awkward honesty, and some
jealousy. • Do not make attraction appear suddenly or reduce her to
romantic competition.

13.6 Lex • Ajaye’s sister and a capable Wayfinder. • Affection is
expressed through competition, familiar insults, protectiveness, and the
ability to hurt each other because they know each other well. • Her
decisions must make sense for the people who rely on her. • Her arc
should not require her to abandon her independence or the Wayfinders.

13.7 Kael • Refined, severe, controlled, and accustomed to control. •
His artificial obsidian Sleeve has red/black seams; use the approved
reference for the exact design. • His appearance and equipment contrast
with Ajaye’s patched gear. • He inherits family myths and fragments of
truth, not complete knowledge. • The corruption exploits his fear and
desire for order, but does not absolve him of responsibility. • His
knowledge and actions must match what he has actually learned at each
point.

13.8 Oscar • Discipline conceals a strong sense of duty. • Do not make
him merely a power-up or exposition device. • His reasons for helping
should be personal and earned.

13.9 Reegan • Anger comes from real harm done to his community. • His
trust should develop gradually; humour can emerge later. • In the
existing outline, he is associated with the Fire Sleeve in Book Four and
lends Fire’s power to help restore the fourth tower. Confirm details
against the approved manuscript and references before using them in page
prompts.

13.10 Jax • Jax is not a starting companion. • If introduced later, the
story must establish why Ajaye encounters or adopts Jax and how Ajaye
cares for the animal in a resource-scarce world. • Jax must not become a
convenient solution to major obstacles or appear by default in scenes.

13.11 Character design authority Approved character model/reference
sheets govern appearance. Text descriptions do not authorise redesigns
of faces, hair, proportions, clothing, accessories, colours, or
distinctive features. If a generated image drifts from the approved
reference, correct it before treating it as usable continuity.

14. CURRENT STORY EVENTS AND CONTINUITY ANCHORS

This section records established or already specified Book One story
beats. It does not mean every generated page is approved canon. The
manuscript and approved pages remain the authorities for the exact scene
order and visual state.

14.1 Before the Fogline • Ajaye trains with Sensei in the settlement. •
He repairs equipment for neighbours, demonstrating practical ingenuity.
• The settlement contains ordinary life adapted to scarcity: a market,
repaired water pump, livestock, patched equipment, and family routines.
• The green-grey Fog is visible beyond the perimeter and signals
environmental danger. • Unusual changes in crops, livestock, water, or
other local signs are uncertain and recent, not yet fully understood.

14.2 Edge-Rock and the Cipher Sleeve • Ajaye scavenges near the
perimeter and discovers an ancient plate and the beta Cipher Sleeve. •
The Sleeve bonds to his arm. • It projects or indicates five tower
signals. • Ajaye’s curiosity outruns caution, and the activation awakens
a dormant defence drone. • His first success relies on observation,
weapon skill, and limited scanning—not instant mastery of the ancient
technology.

14.3 Return home and gate alarm • Ajaye returns to Sensei and Mother and
tries to understand what happened. • Sensei encourages him to
distinguish what he saw from what he assumes. • A symbol hints at
partial recognition but not complete knowledge. • The drone reaches the
settlement and attacks the perimeter. • Ajaye uses staff, knife, and the
Sleeve’s brief weak-point scan to disable it. • The fight damages the
gate and consumes scarce repair parts. • A broken projection shows the
first tower failing and the Fog expanding. • Villagers disagree about
whether Ajaye brought danger home.

14.4 Departure • The settlement debates whether Ajaye should follow the
tower map. • Sensei tests Ajaye’s judgement, not just his fighting
skill. • Mother helps him assemble a plausible travel kit: filters,
water, food, cord, repair wire, first aid, limited trade stock, and
manageable weapons. • Ajaye leaves without Jax. • The journey begins
with real limits on weight, supplies, comfort, and certainty.

14.5 The old transit line • Ajaye travels through an abandoned transit
corridor. • He encounters survivors, route warnings, a filter-repair
stall, and a small market in a station concourse. • He must barter for a
replacement seal instead of finding everything freely. • His Sleeve
cannot connect to market devices without a compatible plug-in or
interface.

14.6 Korb and Wind • Ajaye meets Korb in a dangerous, awkward
predicament involving a cable, a scavenger drone, and Korb’s
overconfidence. • Their first exchange establishes Korb’s jokes as a
defence against fear and loneliness. • Korb’s consumer Sleeve reacts to
Ajaye’s relic. • Through Cipher’s link, Wind recognises Korb as a
potential host. • Korb’s protective action reveals his character, and
Wind chooses him. • Korb’s first Wind use is risky and physically
costly, not controlled mastery. • Ajaye and Korb travel together because
each has something the other lacks; trust develops rather than appearing
instantly.

14.7 Roadside exchange and later route • The pair reach a settlement
with rentable and chargeable compact hover-assisted scooters on suitable
routes. • Transport depends on battery swaps, terrain, local rules, and
maintenance; beyond the maintained route they must walk. • A mechanic
charges them for a repair; Korb’s theatrical haggling reveals his
personality. • They encounter signs and tracks associated with the Rust
faction as the route toward the tower continues.

14.8 First tower • Book One’s objective is to reach and reactivate the
first tower. • Tower restoration improves local conditions rather than
instantly fixing the planet. • The activation is the point at which
Cipher should first genuinely communicate with Ajaye, according to the
current proposed story rule. • The tower reveals or points toward a
wider journey, including the next route toward the Sunken Coast in the
current outline. • Exact page-by-page events and the current state of
the characters’ equipment must be checked against the latest approved
page and continuity record before continuing artwork.

14.9 Approval rule The events above describe manuscript/outline
continuity. A generated image, draft prompt, or unapproved revision is
not approved canon. Do not treat a rejected or inconsistent rendering as
the authoritative appearance of a character or Sleeve.

15. FIVE-BOOK SAGA: HIGH-LEVEL CONTINUITY ONLY

The five-book outline is maintained as a separate working planning
document and should be revised in a later, dedicated task. The following
summary is only to orient worldbuilding; it does not replace the
detailed outline.

BOOK ONE — THE PERIMETER AND THE FIRST TOWER Discovery, departure, the
introduction of the Cipher Sleeve, Korb’s Wind bond, travel through a
resource-scarce world, and restoration of the first tower. The first
restoration produces local improvement and points toward the next part
of the journey.

BOOK TWO — WORKING TITLE / DETAILS TO BE VERIFIED The group expands and
faces the consequences of restoring another system. The story should
show that recovery is not automatically safe and deepen the
relationships and travel realities. Consult the current complete story
outline before relying on individual plot beats.

BOOK THREE — WORKING TITLE / DETAILS TO BE VERIFIED A major reversal
reveals that accepted history of the collapse is incomplete. The ancient
catastrophe, modern collapse, and secret extension project become more
closely connected without resolving every mystery too early.

BOOK FOUR — WORKING TITLE / DETAILS TO BE VERIFIED Kael’s family legacy
and the consequences of the 26th ability become more immediate. Reegan’s
Fire connection and the fourth tower feature in the current outline. The
story examines control, preservation, and cooperation.

BOOK FIVE — THE APEX CITADEL The group reaches the Apex Citadel and
confronts the virus and the question of whether the old system should be
restored unchanged. The current outline proposes a Rebind requiring the
five true Sleeves and willing spirit cooperation. Cipher’s antivirus,
the sixth Dark spirit, and a possible Cipher/Dark counterpart
relationship form part of the endgame. The final result must be earned;
the world does not reset to perfection.

Important: the complete five-book outline is not being rewritten in this
document. A separate revised outline should be created only after this
Bible is reviewed and approved.

16. VISUAL AND PRODUCTION CANON

16.1 Art direction • Full-colour anime/manga graphic novel. • Polished
digital illustration, confident anime linework, painterly environments,
controlled texture, and cinematic light. • Not chibi, not
photorealistic, and not generic Western superhero art. • Everyday world
is circa 2036 and recognisably contemporary. • Ancient technology should
feel like a distinct, advanced, purposeful technological lineage that is
partly inaccessible and difficult to reproduce. • Character designs
should reflect resources and social position. Ajaye’s patched gear and
Kael’s refined, expensive appearance should contrast deliberately.

16.2 Comic page production • Comic-book pages must be portrait format at
exactly 1080 × 1536 pixels. • Verify actual output dimensions; do not
assume the generator’s default is correct. • Follow the approved page
blueprint, panel order, story boundaries, and continuity handoff. • Use
the approved character reference sheets as image inputs whenever
supported. • Inspect generated images for character drift, incorrect
Sleeve placement, wrong colours, changed clothing, altered proportions,
or continuity errors. • A wrong image is a failed continuity check and
must be corrected before it is reused. • Do not repeat scenes, introduce
future events early, or treat an unapproved draft as canon. • Use exact
approved dialogue and captions only. Do not invent consequential
dialogue to fill a gap. • If text rendering is unreliable, generate
clean art and add lettering in a separate layout pass.

16.3 Ajaye Sleeve visual lock Ajaye’s green Cipher Sleeve is on one arm
only. Do not show it on both arms. Match the approved character
reference for exact arm, shape, materials, and attachment. Do not mirror
the reference. If a prompt or composition makes the correct side
unclear, specify it explicitly and use the reference image.

16.4 Korb visual lock Use the approved Korb reference for facial
features, hair, outfit, proportions, age impression, and emerald Wind
Sleeve. Keep his relative height and build consistent with Ajaye; do not
make him appear inexplicably much younger or much shorter.

16.5 Page-to-page continuity Before each page, check: • Correct book,
chapter, page number, and title. • Page beat list and preceding page’s
continuity-out note. • Approved character references. • Character
positions, equipment, Sleeve arm, clothing, props, injuries, time,
weather, and location. • Required panel count and camera progression. •
Only events allowed on this page. • Exact supported captions/dialogue. •
Final panel and continuity-out state against the next page’s
continuity-in note.

17. RULES FOR WRITING AND ADAPTING SCENES

Every scene should satisfy these tests: 1. Does the scene fit the
near-future setting around 2036? 2. If technology works, has the story
established its power, interface, permissions, module, skill, and
physical access? 3. Does Cipher have the app/plugin, data source,
credential, connection, energy, and access needed for the action? 4.
Does the host’s condition affect the ability in a consistent way? 5. Is
the spirit treated as an intelligent agent with its own choices? 6. Does
a plot-critical resource have a credible source? 7. Are the ancient
catastrophe and modern collapse kept distinct unless evidence connects
them? 8. Is information revealed at the right point, rather than
prematurely resolving a mystery? 9. Does the action have consequences
for people, resources, relationships, or reputation? 10. Is a
character’s competence shown without making them instantly correct or
unlimited? 11. Does the scene reflect the latest approved visual and
story continuity? 12. Does the scene avoid relying on an unresolved
module, technology, or lore detail without first defining it?

18. OPEN DECISIONS AND MYSTERIES

The following must remain open unless deliberately resolved and
approved:

ANCIENT HISTORY • Exact cause and date of the ancient catastrophe. •
Exact purpose, engineering, and limits of the original towers. • The
chronology and creators of the original AI and spirits. • What the
ancient civilisation knew about the spirits and modules. • What ordinary
people in 2036 know, believe, or misunderstand about the ancient
civilisation.

MODERN COLLAPSE • The exact causal sequence connecting tower failure,
Darkness-related corruption, AI/cloud outages, environmental-system
failure, and social breakdown. • Which records survived and who can
access them. • The rules and infrastructure of clean-air zones.

SLEEVES AND ENERGY • Exact relationship between consumer Sleeves
launched in 2026 and the ancient ability-bearing Sleeve technology. •
Precise internal energy mechanism, charging method, capacity, recharge
time, and numerical costs. • Which low-level functions consume energy
and how energy use is measured. • Exact thresholds and safeguards for
sleep, unconsciousness, injury, and emergency autonomy. • Whether other
hosts’ Sleeves follow precisely the same host-condition rules as Ajaye’s
beta Sleeve or have simpler implementations.

MODULES AND SPIRITS • Exact list, names, functions, range, cost, and
failure modes of all 25 original modules. • Exact allocation of the 25
modules among the five identities/domains. • How many modules Ajaye can
store, install, or carry simultaneously. • Whether multiple modules can
be active at once. • Compatibility rules and the role of spirit consent
in module use, tower access, and Rebind. • Exact names, appearances, and
personalities of spirits not yet fixed by approved references. • The
exact relationship between Cipher, the original AI, Darkness, the virus,
and the sixth Dark spirit.

THE STORY • Exact events and continuity state for every page not yet
approved. • The detailed route, locations, time, weather, supplies,
injuries, and equipment state across all five books. • The four
uncompleted extensions and whether any partial designs survive. •
Whether Cipher and the Dark spirit can or should become a more complete
intelligence. • Whether the original network can be safely restored, and
what must change to avoid repeating the old failure.

OPEN means not yet decided. Do not fill these gaps with invented canon
simply because a scene would be easier to write with an answer.
